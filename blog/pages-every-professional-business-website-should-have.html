What Pages Should Every Professional Business Website Have?

Not sure what to put on your website? Here are the essential pages every professional business site needs and what each one should contain.

One of the biggest questions when planning a website is simple: what should actually go on it? Add too little and visitors cannot find what they need. Add too much and the site becomes confusing, slow and expensive to maintain.

The good news is that most successful business websites share the same core structure. Below are the pages that matter, why each one exists and what to include, so you can plan with confidence or brief a developer clearly.

1. Home page: your first impression

Your Home page has one job: help a stranger understand who you are, what you do and what to do next, all within a few seconds.

  • A clear headline stating what you offer and where
  • A short supporting line and a main call-to-action button, such as "Call Now" or "Get a Free Quote"
  • Highlights of your top services
  • Trust signals such as years of experience, certifications or client logos
  • Contact details in the header or footer

2. About page: the people behind the business

This is often the second most visited page. Customers want to know who they are dealing with, especially for services built on trust.

  • Your story, mission and what makes you different
  • Photos and short bios of the owner or team
  • Qualifications, licences and years of experience
  • Your values and approach to customers

Keep it human and honest. Avoid vague claims like "best in India" unless you can prove them.

3. Services page(s): what you sell

Describe each service clearly. For businesses with several offerings, give each main service its own page, which helps both visitors and Google. Each page should explain the benefit, the process, who it is for and how to enquire. Our own services page is an example of how to present offerings clearly.

Tip: write for customers, not for yourself

Instead of "We provide comprehensive solutions", say "We repair AC units the same day across South Delhi." Specific beats generic every time.

4. Contact page: make it easy to reach you

  • Phone number (tap-to-call on mobile), email and WhatsApp link
  • Full address with an embedded Google Map and directions
  • Business hours and expected response time
  • A short contact form with name, phone, service and message

Make sure the details match your Google Business Profile exactly, because consistency helps local SEO.

5. FAQ page or section: remove hesitation

List real questions customers ask, such as pricing approach, timelines, service areas, payment methods and what to bring. Good FAQs save you repetitive calls and can help your pages appear for question-style searches. Keep answers short and accurate.

6. Testimonials and case studies: only if genuine

Social proof is powerful, but only when it is real. Use reviews and feedback from actual customers, with their permission, and include a name, location or business where possible. Never invent testimonials, buy fake reviews or exaggerate results; this damages trust and may breach advertising rules.

If you are just starting and do not yet have testimonials, skip the section for now and show your process, qualifications and sample work instead. Add reviews as they arrive.

7. Portfolio, gallery or projects: show your work

For designers, builders, photographers, interior firms, gyms and many service businesses, visual proof sells. Show real before-and-after photos or completed projects with short descriptions of the problem and result. You can see how we present ours on the projects page.

8. Blog or resources: attract searches over time

A blog is optional at launch but valuable. Articles that answer customer questions can bring visitors from Google months or years after publishing. Start small, publish helpful guides and link them to relevant service pages.

9. Legal and policy pages: where applicable

Depending on your business, these pages protect you and reassure customers:

  • Privacy Policy: essential whenever you collect names, phone numbers or emails through forms or analytics. It should explain what you collect and why, in line with Indian data protection requirements.
  • Terms and Conditions: sets expectations for use of your site and services.
  • Refund or Cancellation Policy: important for online sales, bookings and paid services.
  • Shipping Policy and Disclaimers: required for e-commerce or for professionals who need to state limits of advice.

Rules vary by industry, so check requirements with a qualified professional. Our own site includes privacy, terms and refund pages as a reference.

10. Thank-you and custom 404 pages: the small extras

A thank-you page confirms form submissions and helps you track conversions. A friendly 404 page guides visitors back to your main pages instead of leaving them stuck.

A sample structure by business size

Business stageRecommended pages
New / very smallHome, About, Services, Contact (4 pages) plus privacy policy
Growing local businessAdd individual service pages, FAQs, gallery, testimonials, terms (7–10 pages)
Established / multi-serviceAdd blog, team, locations, case studies, refund policy (12+ pages)

Navigation and planning tips

  • Keep the main menu to six or seven items.
  • Use plain labels: "Services", not "What We Do Best".
  • Place your contact button on every page.
  • Link related pages to each other to help visitors and search engines.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum number of pages a business website needs?

Most businesses can start with four core pages: Home, About, Services and Contact. Add a privacy policy as soon as you collect personal data through forms.

Do I need a privacy policy on my website?

If your website collects any personal information, including through contact forms or analytics, a privacy policy is strongly recommended and often legally required. Consult a legal professional for your situation.

Should every service have its own page?

For major services, yes. Dedicated pages are easier for customers to understand and for Google to rank for specific searches.

Can I add more pages later?

Absolutely. A well-planned site is easy to expand with new service pages, a blog or a gallery as your business grows.

What if I do not have testimonials yet?

Leave the section out until you have real ones. Meanwhile, show your process, qualifications and work samples to build trust.
