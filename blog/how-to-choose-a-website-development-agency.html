How to Choose the Right Website Development Agency for Your Business

The right agency saves you money and headaches; the wrong one costs you months. Use this checklist to evaluate portfolios, pricing, ownership and support.

Hiring a website development agency is a big decision for a small business. A good partner delivers a site that brings in enquiries for years. A poor one disappears after launch, delivers a slow, broken site or leaves you without access to your own files. And because the website industry has no entry barriers, quality varies enormously between agencies, freelancers and "one-man studios".

The good news is that you do not need technical knowledge to choose well. You need to ask the right questions and look at the right evidence. Use this checklist before you sign anything.

1. Evaluate their portfolio properly

A portfolio is the most honest evidence of what an agency can deliver. Do not just admire screenshots; open the live websites.

  • Look for relevant work. Have they built sites for businesses like yours, such as clinics, gyms, shops or professionals? You can see ours on the projects page.
  • Check that the sites are live and actually work. Click through pages, forms and buttons.
  • Look for variety and quality, not templates that all look identical.
  • Ask for the client's role. Did the agency design and build it, or only a small part?

If an agency cannot show any live work, treat it as a red flag.

2. Test mobile responsiveness yourself

Most of your visitors will use phones. Open portfolio sites on your own phone and check:

  • Is the text readable without zooming?
  • Do menus, buttons and forms work easily with a thumb?
  • Do images fit the screen and load quickly?

Also resize a browser window on a computer; a responsive site rearranges itself smoothly at every width. If a layout breaks, it is not truly mobile-friendly.

3. Ask about SEO basics

You are not buying SEO miracles, but a website should be built search-friendly from the start. Ask whether the price includes:

  • Proper page titles, meta descriptions and heading structure
  • A sitemap and robots.txt file
  • Clean URLs, image alt text and fast-loading pages
  • Google Business Profile and Search Console setup guidance
  • Schema markup for your business details where relevant

Be cautious of anyone who guarantees "number 1 on Google". No honest agency can promise a ranking, because search engines make that decision.

4. Check performance and speed

Run their portfolio sites through Google PageSpeed Insights. You do not need perfect scores, but pages that take many seconds to load on mobile are a warning. Ask how they optimise images, code and hosting. Our speed optimization service is built around this.

5. Demand pricing transparency

A good quote is clear and itemised. Insist on knowing:

  • What exactly is included: number of pages, revisions, features and integrations
  • What costs extra: additional pages, copywriting, photography and plugins
  • Domain, hosting and renewal costs, and whether GST applies
  • Payment schedule and what happens if the project stalls

The cheapest quote is not always the best. See our breakdown of website costs in India to understand what is realistic. WebInstant publishes its prices openly on the plans page.

6. Clarify ownership before you pay

This is the most overlooked point and the one that causes the worst disputes. Confirm in writing that:

  • The domain is registered in your name, with you as the owner
  • You receive full access to hosting, admin logins and source files
  • Design assets, content and code you paid for belong to you after final payment
  • You can move to another provider if needed without penalties

Never accept an arrangement where the agency holds your domain or logins "for safety".

7. Understand support and maintenance

Websites need updates, backups and quick fixes. Ask:

  • How long is free support after launch?
  • Who handles security updates and backups?
  • What are the monthly or yearly maintenance charges, and what do they cover?
  • How fast do they respond when something breaks?

Clear terms here matter more than fancy promises. Our maintenance and support service shows what a typical plan covers.

8. Judge communication and process

How an agency treats you before the contract is a preview of how it will behave afterwards. Look for prompt replies, clear timelines, a defined process (discovery, design, development, review, launch) and honest answers when you ask hard questions. If they are vague or pushy now, expect worse later.

9. Read genuine reviews and ask for references

Check Google reviews, LinkedIn and social pages, and ask for two or three past clients you can speak to. A confident agency will happily provide them.

A short list of questions to ask on your first call

  1. Can you show me live websites for businesses like mine?
  2. What is included in this price, and what costs extra?
  3. Will the site be mobile-friendly and optimised for speed and SEO?
  4. Who owns the domain, hosting and files at the end?
  5. What support do I get after launch?
  6. How long will the project take?

If the answers are clear, specific and consistent, you are probably talking to a professional. Once you have chosen a partner, avoid the issues listed in 10 common website mistakes that cost businesses customers.

Frequently asked questions

Should I hire a freelancer or an agency?

Both can work. Freelancers are often more affordable for small projects, while agencies offer a team, process and continuity. Judge by portfolio, communication and support, not by label.

How long should a website project take?

A small business website often takes one to two weeks with timely content and feedback. Larger or custom projects take longer, and a good agency will give you a clear timeline up front.

Do I need to give content to the agency?

You will usually provide your logo, business details and photos. Many agencies help organise and polish text, and some offer copywriting as an extra service.

What should be in the contract?

Scope of work, price and payment schedule, timeline, number of revisions, ownership of assets and domain, support period and terms for changes or cancellation.

Is it risky to choose the cheapest agency?

It can be. Very low prices may mean templates, no SEO work, little support or hidden extras. Compare what is included, not only the amount.
